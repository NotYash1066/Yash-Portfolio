I realized I was not learning slowly because I lacked resources.

I was learning slowly because I kept choosing the comfort of watching someone else solve the hard part.

Every time I hit a wall in a project, my first instinct was to find a tutorial that solved the exact same problem. Not to understand the underlying concept — just to get past the wall. And it worked. Temporarily. Until the next wall was slightly different, and the tutorial didn't exist.

So I changed the rule:

**Build first. Search second. Document the confusion before it disappears.**

The shift wasn't dramatic. I didn't suddenly become a genius debugger. But I started noticing patterns in my confusion. I started recognizing *categories* of problems instead of just *instances* of problems.

The tutorials I watch now are different too. I watch them after I've already struggled — so I can compare my approach to theirs. That's when the learning actually compounds.

I still use tutorials. But I stopped letting them replace the struggle. The struggle is the curriculum.
